Three of us parked in Victor Harbor and started walking west from Kings Beach as the clouds were still sitting low over Encounter Bay. It felt like the right kind of morning for a big coastal walk — still, a little moody, the light doing that flat-grey thing that looks terrible on digital and perfect on film.

<div class="pullquote"><p>"The sign reads <em>Nguldi arndu</em> — welcome. It feels genuinely meant."</p></div>

The Wild South Coast Way trailhead signage at Kings Beach marks where the Heysen Trail turns inland and north toward the Flinders Ranges. We took the coast. From here the track immediately heads up and along the Waitpinga Cliffs — one of the most iconic stretches of coastline in South Australia, and it earns that reputation within the first kilometre.

## Kings Beach to the Cliffs

The cliffs here rise close to 100 metres above the Southern Ocean. The track runs right along the edge — not always fenced, not always wide — with views back toward The Bluff and Victor Harbor on one side and open water stretching south toward Antarctica on the other. We burned through film fast.

The track undulates constantly along this section — short sharp climbs to each headland, then a descent toward the next cove. It's not brutal elevation, but your legs notice it. The surface is mostly compacted clay and coastal scrub, with the occasional rocky scramble near the headland points. Keep your footing on the shale sections; it gets slippery when damp.

One of us wore short sleeves. By the time we hit the halfway mark, the sou'westerly off the strait had him thoroughly reconsidering that particular life choice.

## Newland Head & the Cliffline

The standout moment is the high point above Newland Head, where the full sweep of the Waitpinga Cliffline is visible — a long, unbroken wall of orange and grey rock dropping sheer to the sea. West Island sits clearly offshore to the south, close enough to make out its shape. On a clear day you can see well down the coastline; under our flat autumn cloud cover the light was moody but the views no less impressive.

The geology along this section is extraordinary — layered bands of orange, rust and ochre in the cliff faces, almost painted. A few accessible cove scrambles exist if you're willing to burn 20 minutes. We found one and dropped down for lunch on the rocks — complete solitude, small waves, perfect.

## Parsons Beach to Waitpinga

The final stretch descends from the cliffs to Parsons Beach — a wide, exposed surf beach with powerful dumping waves. The track continues along the beach and over the sand dunes into the Waitpinga area, where the walk ends near the campground. The transition from exposed clifftop to sheltered dune is sudden and welcome.

<div class="pullquote"><p>"The Waitpinga Cliffs are one of South Australia's most photographed coastal landmarks — and standing on them, you understand why."</p></div>

We finished at the Waitpinga Campground carpark. Shuttle logistics are straightforward with two cars — leave one here, drive to the Kings Beach carpark in Victor Harbor to start. Total loop with the drive is about 40 minutes.

## Conditions & Notes

We walked on an overcast autumn morning — 14°C, moderate southwesterly, occasional light rain. The light was flat but ideal for photography, especially on film. The exposed cliff sections are consistently windy; a windproof layer is essential regardless of how warm it looks at the trailhead. The track is well-maintained and signposted throughout with Heysen Trail blazes. No technical sections, but some scrambles near the headlands require care. May–October is also whale season — keep an eye on the water for southern right whales and calves.
